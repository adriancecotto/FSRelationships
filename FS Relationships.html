1. Retained Earnings & Dividend Payout Mechanics

Net Income from the IS is allocated between Cash Dividends and Retained Earnings. Dividends can be determined via the target Dividend Payout Ratio or by solving the Retained Earnings rollforward.

Dividend Payout Ratio = Dividends Declared / Net Income Ending Retained Earnings = Beginning RE + Net Income – Dividends Declared

Numerical Proof (FY 2025):

Dividends Declared = $240.0M Net Income × 20.83% = $50.0M
Ending RE = $230.0M (2024 RE) + $240.0M (NI) – $50.0M (Dividends) = $420.0M

2. CapEx & Net/Gross PP&E Rollforward

Capital Expenditures represent additions to fixed assets. CapEx can be calculated using either Gross PP&E changes or Net PP&E adjusted for Depreciation and Asset Disposals.

CapEx = Gross PP&E 2025 – Gross PP&E 2024 + Gross Cost of Assets Sold CapEx = Net PP&E 2025 – Net PP&E 2024 + Depreciation Expense + Net Book Value Sold

Numerical Proof (FY 2025):

CapEx = $825M (Net PP&E 2025) – $700M (Net PP&E 2024) + $70M (Dep) + $5M (NBV Sold) = $200M

3. Debt Rollforward & Financing Flow Integration

The net change in debt on the Balance Sheet is the resultant vector of gross principal issuances and principal repayments made during the fiscal year.

Ending Debt = Beginning Debt + New Debt Issued – Debt Repayments New Debt Issued = Ending Debt – Beginning Debt + Debt Repayments

Numerical Proof (FY 2025):

$520.0M (2025 Debt) = $450.0M (2024 Debt) + $100.0M (New Debt) – $30.0M (Repaid)
New Debt Issued = $520M – $450M + $30M = $100.0M

4. Cash Reconciliation Tie-Out (CFS ➔ BS)

The Cash Flow Statement provides the detailed cash activity explaining the period-over-period movement in the Balance Sheet Cash account.

Ending Cash (BS 2025) = Beginning Cash (BS 2024) + Net Cash Flow (NCF) NCF = Operating Cash Flow (CFO) + Investing Cash Flow (CFI) + Financing Cash Flow (CFF)

Numerical Proof (FY 2025):

$100.0M (Cash 2024) + [$265M (CFO) – $180M (CFI) + $70M (CFF)] = $255.0M (Cash 2025)

🏛️ US GAAP (ASC 230) Standards: Location & Presentation of Taxes and Interest

1. Why are Interest Expense and Income Tax Expenses kept inside Net Income rather than listed below it in Indirect CFO?
Under US GAAP (ASC 230), the Indirect Method begins with Net Income. Because Net Income already incorporates Interest Expense ($-40M) and Income Tax Expense ($-80M) on an accrual basis, no adjustments are needed in the CFO reconciliation line items unless there are unpaid accrued taxes/interest on the Balance Sheet. Placing them separately under Net Income in the statement would double-count them.

2. Required Supplemental Disclosures:
US GAAP strictly requires that entities using the Indirect Method disclose the actual Cash Paid for Interest and Cash Paid for Income Taxes either at the bottom of the Cash Flow Statement or in the financial statement footnotes. Both MUST remain classified within Cash Flow from Operating Activities (CFO).

IFRS Comparison: Under IFRS (IAS 7), Interest Paid can be classified as Operating or Financing, and Taxes Paid can be classified as Investing/Financing if directly attributable to specific asset transactions.

📖 Case Study: GlobalTech Industries (FY 2025 Operational Context)
Executive Summary for Students:
GlobalTech Industries is a mid-sized advanced manufacturing enterprise undergoing a strategic production capacity expansion during FY 2025. This case study demonstrates how operational earnings, working capital investments, capital asset acquisitions, and capital structure financing decisions interconnect across all three financial statements.

Key Operating & Financial Highlights (2025):

  • Operating Performance: Generated $1,500M in net revenues and achieved $240M in Net Income after accounting for $70M in Depreciation and $80M in Income Taxes.
  • Capital Expansion (CapEx): Invested $200M in new automated equipment to scale manufacturing capacity. Concurrently, retired obsolete equipment with an original cost of $20M (accumulated depreciation of $15M, Net Book Value of $5M) for $20M cash, realizing a $5M gain on sale.
  • Working Capital Investment: Operations required cash absorption into working capital, as Accounts Receivable expanded by $40M and Inventories grew by $30M, partially offset by a $30M increase in Accounts Payable financing.
  • Capital Raising & Distribution: Financed growth through a $100M new long-term bond issuance and $60M in equity capital, repaid $30M of existing debt principal, and paid $60M in total cash dividends ($10M declared in prior period + $50M declared in 2025).
Learning Objective: Trace how $240M in accrual Net Income translates into $265M in Operating Cash Flow, $155M in Net Cash Expansion, and a final Balance Sheet cash balance of $255M.
